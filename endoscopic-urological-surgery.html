Endoscopic urological surgery

Endoscopic procedures use a small camera and instruments to examine or treat parts of the urinary tract. The exact method depends on the diagnosis and planned procedure.

Ask about an evaluation
Endoscopic care

Understanding the procedure

For many endoscopic operations, instruments are passed through the natural urinary opening; some procedures use a small access route instead. The clinician explains what is planned, whether anaesthesia is needed, and if a temporary catheter or stent may be part of care.

Preparation and risk discussion

Beforehand, review medicines, allergies, past anaesthesia, and test results with the team. Possible risks depend on the operation and can include infection, bleeding, discomfort, or a need for additional treatment. Ask which risks apply to your case.

Aftercare

Instructions are specific to the procedure. The team should explain how to manage discomfort, when normal activities may resume, whether follow-up testing is planned, and which symptoms need urgent attention.

Before you decide

A consultation helps match the intended procedure to the reason for treatment and review other available options.
