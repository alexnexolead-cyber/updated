Kidney Care & Kidney Stones

Kidney or ureteric stones may cause pain, blood in the urine, nausea, or difficulty passing urine. Symptoms vary, and an examination is needed to understand what is causing them.

Kidney & stone care
Assessment and options

Evaluation of a suspected stone

A clinician may ask where discomfort occurs, how it began, whether you have had stones before, and whether fever or urinary symptoms are present. Urine tests, blood tests, or imaging such as ultrasound or CT may be considered according to the situation.

Care depends on the stone and your health

Some stones can be monitored while symptoms and passage are followed. Other cases may need a procedure. Size, location, infection, kidney function, and your medical history inform the discussion; PCNL is one procedure used for selected kidney stones and has its own assessment and recovery considerations.

When to seek urgent care

Fever or chills with suspected stones, inability to pass urine, uncontrolled pain, persistent vomiting, or feeling seriously unwell warrants urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for a routine appointment if symptoms are severe.

Before your consultation

Bring available scan or laboratory reports and your current medicine list. Ask how any proposed plan may affect follow-up, hydration advice, and prevention based on your stone evaluation.
